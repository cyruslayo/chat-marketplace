# Panel 5: "The opposite of a good idea" (contrarian / psychological moonshots)

Lens: find the apparent drawback of Shortlet and present it as the point. Every option is within its slot limit (character counts checked). `⚠` marks copy that leans on a fact the brief does not fully settle.

## 1. Insight

- The visitor is most afraid of being strung along: an agent who goes quiet on WhatsApp, a price that appears only at the end, and paying for a place that has already gone.
- Speed is not what they are buying. A fast promise ("instant") would make them suspicious. A stated wait with a stated deadline reads as care, and a sign that someone is actually checking.
- Fewer results feels like less choice. Framed as "only what you can request", it removes the work of checking each listing themselves.
- The Operator confirming before payment is a costly signal: it adds friction, and that friction is evidence the apartment is real and the dates are being checked.
- Nothing happens until they press send. That is a relief to someone who has been burned by messages going out before they were ready.
- Flag for the FAQ owner: the Q1 answer ("No. The dates are yours once the Operator confirms…") sits next to the 30-minute hold in B4 and H4. The copy here does not change that answer, but the two should be reconciled before launch.

## 4. One bold idea

**Make the wait a section, not a footnote.** Replace the plain "How it works" framing with a short band above the five beats titled "Five things Shortlet does on purpose": you describe the trip in your own words; you see only what you can request; nothing is sent until you press send; the Operator says yes before any payment; your dates are held while they reply, during their active hours, for up to 30 minutes; starting a new conversation cancels nothing you've sent. Every line is a fact from the brief, but the list turns the drawbacks (fewer results, a wait, a typed message) into the deliberate design the visitor is buying into.
