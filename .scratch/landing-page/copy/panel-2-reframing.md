# Panel 2: Reframing and contrast

## Insight

- The visitor's real fear is paying for something that then isn't there: money gone, dates already taken, a price that grows after they've committed. Losses land about twice as hard as gains, so the copy should make the moment money moves, and the number they'll see, feel certain.
- Today's experience has no visible end: a WhatsApp thread, "price on request", and fees that surface at the last step. Contrast works when a line names the status-quo moment and then gives the calmer version of the same fact.
- Reframe "checking" as "nothing leaves until you say so". The same facts (nothing sent until you press send, price re-checked at send, payment only after confirmation) become a feeling of control instead of a list of procedures.
- Don't overpromise. The deposit sits outside the total, the Operator can decline, and the pilot has no online ID check. A "no surprises" claim would break the trust the page is trying to earn, so every contrast here is built on a fact in the brief.
