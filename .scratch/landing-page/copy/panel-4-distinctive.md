# Panel 4: Distinctive and memorable

Lens: Rory Sutherland's preference for the distinctive and memorable over the merely logical. Small, surprising, human lines that a visitor would repeat to a friend. Every line below is checked against the product facts in the brief.

Note on the current copy: S02 (the current H1, 77 characters) is already over its 70 limit. The options below fix that.

## Insight

- The visitor is not mainly worried about speed. They are worried about being managed by strangers at the money moments: a price that moves, a deposit that appears, a payment that goes nowhere. Certainty is the product.
- The status quo is tedium and small humiliation (retyping the same trip, chasing an agent on WhatsApp). A little wit about the chore earns attention; a claim about the product does not.
- The line worth repeating is the sequence itself: say it once, see the full price, pay after the Operator says yes. If a visitor remembers one sentence, it should be that one.
- Wit stays away from numbers, deposits and payment. Those lines stay plain, because that is where the trust is decided.
- The answers to Q4 and Q5 are still placeholders in the markup. Copy near them is kept neutral until the approved answers exist.
