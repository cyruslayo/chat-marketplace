# Panel 3: Costly signals and trust

Lens: a visitor trusts a claim more when the business visibly gives something up to make it: refusing to send, refusing to charge early, refusing to show what it cannot book, and admitting limits. Shortlet already has several such rules. The copy should present each rule as a promise the product keeps, not as a feature.

Character counts are against the limits in the brief. ⚠ marks a line that leans on a fact the brief does not fully settle.

## 1. Insight

- **The fear is being committed without choosing.** The visitor is afraid of a send they did not mean, a charge before the apartment is real, and a place that has already gone. Shortlet's strongest trust assets are the things it refuses to do: send without the press of a button, take money before an Operator says yes, show anything it cannot book, and charge nothing if the Operator declines. Copy should name those refusals in the visitor's own terms.
- **Specific, bounded numbers read as costly.** "30 minutes", "up to 14 nights", "10:45 am WAT" and "Lagos and Abuja" are hard to fake because they can be checked. Honest limits ("for now", "this pilot", "not online") do the same job. A visitor who is told what the product does not do is more likely to believe what it does.
- **Risk 1: the hold and Q1 contradict each other.** The current Q1 answer begins "No." But the beat 4 banner says dates are held for up to 30 minutes while the Operator replies. A visitor who notices will doubt the whole page. The fix keeps every fact and states the hold plainly (see Q1). Needs owner sign-off because it changes the answer wording.
- **Risk 2: "all-in" can look broken at the payment step.** The offer screen shows "Amount due now" as the All-In Stay Total plus the Refundable Security Deposit. So "all-in" is not "everything you pay now". Any copy that says "the price is what you pay" will be caught out. Always name the deposit in the same sentence as the total.
- **Risk 3: slot limits and dates.** The current H1 (S02) is 77 characters against a limit of 70. The example dates (10–13 Sept, reply due 3 Sept 2026) are in the past as of 12 Oct 2026. Neither is my slot to fix, but both undermine a page about trust; flagging for the owner.

## 4. Bold idea

Replace the closing "Where are you headed?" block with a short panel titled "What we won’t do": send without your press, take payment before the Operator says yes, show an apartment you can’t book, and leave the deposit off the total. Close the panel with the honest limits as a list ("Lagos and Abuja, for now. Stays up to 14 nights. No online identity check in this pilot."). This turns the product's rules into self-imposed promises that a visitor can check, and it puts the honest limits where the trust is decided, not in a footnote.
