Monthly stays · Lagos & Abuja

Monthly Stay Supply Strategy

How we structure deals with apartment operators, why they hold, and where pricing creates value for operators, guests and the platform.

4 Oct 2026Status: draft for decisionFollows ADR 0089, ADR 0092Issues 15 & 16

1The scarce thing isn't apartments

Lagos has too many shortlets. Airbnb occupancy is about 43% and revenue per listing is falling. About 24% of stays already run 30 nights or more. Two things are scarce:

  • Supply you can trust for a month: inspected, powered, with a real manager and fixed terms.
  • Qualified monthly demand: assignment professionals and relocators who can pay one month at a time.

Whoever brings the demand leads the deal. We aren't asking operators for apartments. We're selling them occupancy certainty, which they can't produce alone because we pool demand across many units.

The trade in every deal is the same: the operator gives up some price, and we give them certainty. It works like insurance. We can price the risk because we see demand across the whole portfolio.

2Does the trade work for the operator?

Compare the operator's expected net income from nightly letting with the certain net income from a monthly stay. Utilities matter more than they first appear. A monthly price includes utilities (ADR 0092) and the unit is occupied every night, so the operator buys diesel for about 17 more nights than at 43% occupancy. Gross nightly income overstates the comparison.

3The system as a network

The nodes are operators, guests, the employers who reimburse guests, estate agents who serve relocators when they sign a lease, and service vendors (diesel, cleaning, internet). Five loops decide whether the network compounds or leaks.

4Keeping both sides on the platform

A monthly stay is a repeated game with an easy way out. The guest stays 90 days and has the owner's number in week one. What keeps them with us, strongest first:

  1. Value that renews every Billing Period. An employer-accepted receipt, payment protection, the mid-stay remedy matrix, maintenance handling and human support. Going direct usually means losing the reimbursable receipt.
  2. The whole stay is contracted at once, and extensions are never agreed off-platform (ADR 0092).
  3. A margin that makes defection not worth it. If the saving from going direct is small, the hassle wins.
  4. A non-circumvention promise for platform-introduced guests, about 12 months. It deters more than it gets enforced. Counsel must word it.
  5. Benefits operators lose if they leave: shared-services pricing, the account manager, eligibility for future guarantee terms.

Our own commitments must be credible too. Published ranking rules (ADR 0066) and a published enforcement ladder (ADR 0064) are sales material: operators can predict how we'll treat them.

5The operator tier menu

Instead of negotiating each deal blind, we offer a short menu and let each operator choose. The choice shows whether they value calendar control, price or certainty. Tiers are proposals for issue 15, not policy.

GuaranteedDeepest discount; monthly stays only through us; revenue share above a floorA quarterly revenue floor, topped up if we don't fill the unitNew ADR
What the ADRs rule out. Three ideas from the first conversation don't survive the ADR check:
  • Priority placement for a tier: ADR 0066 bans sponsored placement. Committed units can rank better only by actually being more reliable.
  • Faster payout as a benefit: ADR 0089 already pays 24 hours after Verified Access, which is the guest-protection timing. Paying faster would weaken protection.
  • Exclusivity or rate parity: ADR 0039 keeps inventory non-exclusive. Both need an ADR change and a competition-law check.

Recommendation: pilot Listed and Committed only. A revenue floor is partly the master lease we rejected on 3 Oct. Offer Guaranteed only after 3 months of fill data in a neighbourhood, with a cap on total exposure.

6Dynamic pricing, sized for monthly stays

Airline-style pricing needs perishable stock, many transactions and short lead times. Monthly stays have few transactions, long lead times, and prices fixed at confirmation (ADR 0092 never reprices a confirmed period). Pricing therefore happens at quote time, through a few levers you can explain to an operator:

  • Length of stay: different rates for 30, 60 and 90 nights.
  • Season: the Dec–Feb diaspora surge, Q1 assignment and relocation starts, July–August.
  • Gap-filling: a discount on a vacancy starting within 14 days, before those nights are lost.
  • Area demand vs supply: a premium where qualified requests outnumber available units.

The goal is revenue per available night across the portfolio, not the price of any single booking. An empty unit at a good price is a loss. Start with rules. Machine learning can wait until there are hundreds of stays.

Floor, ceiling and shared upside

The operator sets a floor (their agreed amount, so they never get less). Together we agree a ceiling. The platform prices within that band, and revenue above a baseline is split, for example 50/50. The operator is protected on the downside, the platform is rewarded for pricing well, and both sides gain by cooperating. This needs a new ADR: today ADR 0089 fixes the guest price as agreed amounts plus margin.

Competition-law risk. Collecting prices from competing operators and setting them centrally can look like algorithmic price coordination. Regulators challenged RealPage in the US over this, and Nigeria's FCCPC could take the same view under the FCCPA 2018. Operators should set floors independently, never see each other's prices, and the platform should price as an agent. This belongs in the counsel review (issue 02).

7How we'll know it's working

Ordered by how strongly each signal shows product-market fit:

  1. Extension rate: guests adding another Billing Period.
  2. Operators committing more units or moving up a tier without being pushed.
  3. Leakage rate: stays that don't come back for the next period.
  4. Employer reimbursement acceptance of our receipts.
  5. Chat-to-stay conversion, and unmet requests by neighbourhood.
