Placeholder illustrations stand in for photography on brand and marketing surfaces until real listings and their photos exist, and they never pretend to show a real place.

**Use for:**
- the landing page;
- mode empty states and introductions;
- campaign pages;
- demo builds.

**Never use for:** a specific apartment, event, group or service. Those show the truthful fallback ("Photo coming soon") with every fact visible, so no illustration is mistaken for the place itself (value 1, Honest).

**Style:**
- **Cut-paper architecture.** Flat shapes built from the radius scale, with no gradients, shadows, outlines on faces or photoreal detail.
- **Colours.** Three per scene, plus ground:
  - `indigo` for structure;
  - the mode accent for the subject;
  - `sand` for light;
  - `canvas` for the openings.
  - The ground is the mode's `*-subtle` tint.
- **No faces or figures.** People are suggested by objects: chairs, keys, a stage. This avoids caricature and any question of consent.
- **Subjects:**
  - Stays: a window, a sofa and a lamp;
  - Events: a stage arch and lights;
  - Community: chairs around a table;
  - Services: a door with keys and a tag.
- **No AI-generated photoreal images.** They look like real places that don't exist.

**The consumer provides:**
- the mode, which sets the `--a` and `--t` tokens;
- an `aria-label` describing the scene, or `alt=""` if the illustration is decorative;
- the visible "Illustration" tag wherever the illustration sits near listing facts.

**Placeholder photos.** Stock or sample photos may appear only in internal demo builds, labelled "Sample photo", and must be removed before public launch (open decision 9).
